# Existing languages leave the circuit-protocol bridge unbuilt

No existing specification language or verification framework gives clocked circuits and asynchronous, parameterized, fault-tolerant protocols one semantics, relates the two by machine-checked refinement, and has every automated step emit evidence that one small kernel checks. Each piece exists, but in tools whose foundational choices conflict: refinement as implication between stuttering-closed behaviours (TLA+), a failures refinement that every composition operator preserves (CSP with FDR), decidable verification conditions for any number of nodes (Ivy), compositional refinement over method calls for hardware (Kami, Fjfj), and contextual refinement for layered kernels (CertiKOS). Across the five design axes the decisive lesson is that the choice of observable, together with its treatment of stuttering, divergence and fairness, quietly fixes what refinement preserves, which compositions are sound and what a certificate must contain. Most machine-checked refinements surveyed preserve safety only, and the end-to-end Lightbulb stack cannot prove that its processor ever executes an instruction ([Erbsen et al.](https://adam.chlipala.net/papers/LightbulbPLDI21/LightbulbPLDI21.pdf)). Certificates are mature only for SAT and for bit-level hardware safety, where the hardware model checking competition has required witness circuits since 2024 and found 44 invalid among 1,536 ([Froleyks et al.](https://cca.informatik.uni-freiburg.de/papers/FroleyksYuPreinerBiereHeljanko-CAV25.pdf)); the theories, quantifiers and liveness that word-level and every-N proofs need remain uncertified. Hardware-to-software stacks checked end to end exist only inside Coq or HOL4, for small systems, at a cost of person-years, and end in trusted HDL toolchains; none reaches a distributed protocol. AI already produces checked proofs and does best inside symbolic loops, where an LLM driven by IC3 found invariants for 29 of 29 protocol benchmarks against 23 on its own ([Cao et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.24619)), but no work proposes refinement mappings between layers, and nothing checks that a specification means what its authors intended. The sections below set out each axis's options, what they bought and what they cost, and the open problems, as considerations for the team to weigh rather than a recommended design.

## Four traditions answer the same questions with incompatible defaults

The surveyed systems grew out of four traditions, each organised around a different first question. **Protocol specification languages** (TLA+ with its Quint surface, Ivy, P and Event-B) ask whether a design is right before code exists. **Process algebras and model checkers** (CSP with FDR, mCRL2, Promela with SPIN, Alloy) ask whether a finite model satisfies a property or refines another model. **Hardware languages and flows** (Bluespec, Kami, Kôika, Fjfj, Chisel with FIRRTL and CIRCT, SystemVerilog Assertions with Cadence Jasper, and ISA-level checking with ISA-Formal, Sail and riscv-formal) ask whether RTL implements its architecture. **Verified-systems frameworks** (IronFleet, Verdi, Verus, Armada, seL4, CertiKOS, Perennial with Grove, Igloo) ask whether shipped code refines a specification. A language for circuits and decentralized protocols inherits a question from each tradition, and none of the traditions' answers was designed to meet the others.

Each tradition is strong exactly where its core semantic choice is strong, and weak in the same place. TLA+ is untyped and closed under stuttering, which makes refinement plain implication, yet every tool narrows it: TLC does not handle the hiding operator and departs from the semantics of `CHOOSE` and strings, and Apalache supports only finite sets, no recursion and no `CHOOSE` ([Lamport](https://lamport.azurewebsites.net/tla/book-02-08-08.pdf); [Apalache](https://apalache-mc.org/docs/apalache/features.html)). Ivy confines verification conditions to decidable logic and so gets predictable automation and counter-models, at the price of reshaping models to fit ([Ivy](http://microsoft.github.io/ivy/decidability.html)). P is a programming language engineers adopt, but its main checker finds bugs without proving their absence ([P](https://p-org.github.io/P/whatisP/)). Event-B attaches explicit proof obligations to every refinement step but carries only safety through the chain ([Stock et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.19069)). Industry has felt both pulls. AWS chose TLA+ over Alloy because it "could not find a practical way in Alloy to represent rich data structures such as dynamic sequences containing nested records", and its engineers learned TLA+ "from scratch" in **2 to 3 weeks** ([Newcombe et al.](https://lamport.azurewebsites.net/tla/formal-methods-amazon.pdf)); since 2019 it has also developed P, "more of a programming language than a mathematical modelling language", as a strategic project ([P](https://p-org.github.io/P/whatisP/); [Brooker & Desai](https://cacm.acm.org/practice/systems-correctness-practices-at-amazon-web-services/)). Hardware shows the same tension between automation and generality. Arm runs thousands of small bounded checks because theorem proving's "reliance on verification experts leads to severe human-scaling issues" ([Reid et al.](https://alastairreid.github.io/papers/cav2016_isa_formal.pdf)), while Kami and Fjfj obtain parametric, compositional theorems in Coq at large manual cost, with Fjfj's examples taking 0.5 to 9 person-months each ([Kami](http://adam.chlipala.net/papers/KamiICFP17/KamiICFP17.pdf); [Fjfj](https://adam.chlipala.net/papers/FjfjPLDI25/FjfjPLDI25.pdf)).

None of these systems was built to span circuits and decentralized protocols under a small kernel, so each design is tuned to a different problem; what transfers is mechanisms and their measured costs, not notations. ## Five design axes, each fixing more than it appears to

The five axes look separable but are coupled. The observable decides which stance on stuttering and divergence is available; that stance decides which compositions preserve refinement and which liveness claims survive; and the composition theory decides what a certificate must contain. Each subsection gives the options the field has tried and what they bought and cost, and ends with the questions they leave open for a language spanning circuits and protocols. A table at the end of the section sets the options side by side.

### What a claim observes decides what its refinement can preserve

Four kinds of observable are in use. **State-based** systems observe variable values. TLA+ observes a formula's free variables, hides the rest by temporal existential quantification, and defines implementation as implication, `Sys ⇒ Spec`, under a refinement mapping that substitutes state functions for the specification's internal variables ([Lamport §5.8, §8.8](https://lamport.azurewebsites.net/tla/book-02-08-08.pdf)). Event-B relates abstract and concrete variables through gluing invariants and splits the data-refinement obligation into guard strengthening, simulation and invariant preservation ([Hoang §7](https://www.southampton.ac.uk/~tsh2n14/publications/chapters/eventb-dbook13.pdf)); seL4 relates states at kernel entry and exit ([seL4](https://www.sigops.org/s/conferences/sosp/2009/papers/klein-sosp09.pdf)); Armada accepts any transitive relation the user writes between implementation and specification states ([Armada](https://www.andrew.cmu.edu/user/bparno/papers/armada.pdf)). **Label-based** systems observe interactions. CSP compares traces, refusals and divergences ([FDR manual](https://dl.cocotec.io/fdr/fdr-manual.pdf)); P's module refinement is trace containment projected onto visible events ([ModP](https://ankushdesai.github.io/assets/papers/modp.pdf)); Kami's labels are method calls paired with their definitions, which its authors present as the hardware counterpart of sends and receives on channels ([Kami §3.2](http://adam.chlipala.net/papers/KamiICFP17/KamiICFP17.pdf)); Verdi observes only external input and output, so messages between nodes stay internal ([Verdi](https://homes.cs.washington.edu/~mernst/pubs/verify-distsystem-pldi2015.pdf)); CertiKOS gives each layer its own observable events ([CertiKOS](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/conference/osdi16/osdi16-gu.pdf)). **Signal-per-cycle** flows observe wire values at clock edges, as in SVA properties and Jasper's sequential equivalence checking of two RTL models ([Reid et al.](https://alastairreid.github.io/papers/cav2016_isa_formal.pdf); [Cadence SEC](https://www.cadence.com/ko_KR/home/tools/system-design-and-verification/formal-and-static-verification/jasper-verification-platform/jaspergold-sequential-equivalence-checking-app.html)); CIRCT's `verif.refines`, the open hardware IR's refinement operation, compares combinational circuits only and supports no registers or memories ([CIRCT](https://circt.llvm.org/docs/Dialects/Verif/)). **Retirement and transaction projections** sit between. ISA-Formal extracts architectural state immediately before and after each instruction through a hand-written abstraction function, riscv-formal requires the core to emit one packet per retired instruction, and Jasper's C2RTL app checks a C model against pipelined RTL transaction by transaction ([Reid et al.](https://alastairreid.github.io/papers/cav2016_isa_formal.pdf); [RVFI](https://yosyshq.readthedocs.io/projects/riscv-formal/en/latest/rvfi.html); [Cadence C2RTL](https://www.cadence.com/en_US/home/tools/system-design-and-verification/formal-and-static-verification/jasper-c-formal-verification.html)). **No surveyed system combines label, cycle and retirement observation in one notion of refinement.**

The observable fixes what a theorem can mean. Refinement as inclusion of finite traces, or as a state relation over finite behaviours, preserves **safety only**: Armada says so outright, and Verdi, Perennial, Grove and Igloo prove safety only ([Armada §8](https://www.andrew.cmu.edu/user/bparno/papers/armada.pdf); [Verdi](https://homes.cs.washington.edu/~mernst/pubs/verify-distsystem-pldi2015.pdf); [Perennial](https://pdos.csail.mit.edu/papers/perennial:sosp19.pdf); [Grove](https://pdos.csail.mit.edu/papers/grove:sosp23.pdf); [Igloo](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.04749)). Lightbulb's whole-system theorem leaves timing unspecified, so "the Kami processor (according to the proofs alone) is not guaranteed ever to execute an instruction – we just tested that it does" ([Erbsen et al. §6](https://adam.chlipala.net/papers/LightbulbPLDI21/LightbulbPLDI21.pdf)), and an AWS lock-manager model "failed to find a liveness bug as we did not check liveness" ([Newcombe et al.](https://lamport.azurewebsites.net/tla/formal-methods-amazon.pdf)). Liveness survives refinement only where behaviours are infinite and fairness is explicit. IronFleet proves that a client eventually gets a reply if the network is eventually synchronous for a live quorum, Anvil states eventual reconciliation as a TLA formula under the assumption that faults eventually stop, and CertiKOS's termination-sensitive refinement covers starvation-freedom ([IronFleet](https://www.andrew.cmu.edu/user/bparno/papers/ironfleet.pdf); [Anvil](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/osdi24-sun-xudong.pdf); [CCAL](https://www.cs.columbia.edu/~rgu/publications/pldi18-gu.pdf)). Event-B carries safety through its refinement chain while liveness is re-validated by animation at each level; a 2025 proposal adds failure-divergence refinement that preserves many liveness properties "under certain natural conditions" ([Stock et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.19069)). In the CSP family the observation model is the dial: traces see only safety, stable failures add what a process may refuse after a trace (where deadlock and offered liveness live), and failures-divergences add divergence as catastrophic ([FDR manual](https://dl.cocotec.io/fdr/fdr-manual.pdf)). At the strong end, CertiKOS's contextual refinement quantifies over every context, so a layer's specification is a drop-in replacement; in the concurrent setting it "is precisely equivalent to the linearizability and progress requirements" ([CertiKOS](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/conference/osdi16/osdi16-gu.pdf)).

Where the boundary sits matters as much as its kind. IronFleet's top-level relation constrains only the packets a system has sent, which brings network traffic into the theorem ([IronFleet §3.1](https://www.andrew.cmu.edu/user/bparno/papers/ironfleet.pdf)); Verdi's external-I/O traces hide protocol messages, and that hiding is what lets a verified transformer add sequence numbers or replication while keeping the specification ([Verdi §4](https://homes.cs.washington.edu/~mernst/pubs/verify-distsystem-pldi2015.pdf)). Kami had to invent a synthetic `tohost` method because "without it, SC would have no externally observable behaviors, which would make for underwhelming final correctness theorems" ([Kami §6.1](http://adam.chlipala.net/papers/KamiICFP17/KamiICFP17.pdf)). Instrumented observation is itself an obligation: riscv-formal checks that the core behaves the same with and without its RVFI port ([riscv-formal](https://yosyshq.readthedocs.io/projects/riscv-formal/en/latest/procedure.html)). A closed specification is not a contract either; Lamport notes that the open-system form `E -+-> M` is "not equivalent" to `E ∧ M` and is what a contract between user and implementer requires ([Lamport §10.9](https://lamport.azurewebsites.net/tla/book-02-08-08.pdf)). When no refinement mapping exists, the standard remedy adds history and prophecy variables ([Lamport's publications](https://lamport.azurewebsites.net/pubs/pubs.html)), and Armada lists the absence of prophecy among its limits ([Armada §8](https://www.andrew.cmu.edu/user/bparno/papers/armada.pdf)).

For a language spanning circuits and protocols, the evidence breaks the choice of observable into separate commitments: which vocabulary is visible (states, labelled interactions, cycle signals or retirement events); whether behaviours are infinite, so that liveness can be stated at all; whether stuttering is closed off in the semantics; whether refusals and divergence are observed; and whether refinement quantifies over contexts. Kami's call-and-definition labels behave like process-algebra channels, so one label vocabulary can describe module ports and network messages alike, with synchronous designs written as a single rule that calls every method, "though with more unwieldy proofs" ([Kami §9](http://adam.chlipala.net/papers/KamiICFP17/KamiICFP17.pdf)); cycle-exact and retirement claims then become special cases with their own costs. Event-B is a caution about deferring the choice: its proof obligations came first, and their behavioural meaning was reconstructed in papers in 2011, 2014 and 2025 ([Schneider et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1106.4098); [Stock et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.19069)). For a kernel that checks first-order steps one property of denotational observation models stands out: traces, failures and simulation relations are sets, so refinement becomes set inclusion and the relation itself can serve as the certificate. That is an inference from the models, not a feature any surveyed tool offers.

### Stuttering, hiding and obligations reconcile step sizes, each with a fairness default

A coarse specification must accept a fine-grained implementation, and the field uses three mechanisms plus one way around the problem. **Stuttering built into the semantics** is TLA+'s answer: `[Next]_v` permits steps that leave v unchanged, so extra implementation steps map to stutters with no further machinery ([Lamport §2.2](https://lamport.azurewebsites.net/tla/book-02-08-08.pdf)). Hardware and systems proofs adopt the same idea. Kami's `EmptyRule` maps implementation transitions that touch only low-level state to empty specification transitions, Lightbulb's hardware-to-software simulation has a stay-in-place disjunct, and IronFleet lets one low-level step map to zero or several high-level steps ([Kami §3.2](http://adam.chlipala.net/papers/KamiICFP17/KamiICFP17.pdf); [Erbsen et al. §5.8](https://adam.chlipala.net/papers/LightbulbPLDI21/LightbulbPLDI21.pdf); [IronFleet §2.1](https://www.andrew.cmu.edu/user/bparno/papers/ironfleet.pdf)). The cost lands on properties. SPIN's partial-order reduction is valid only for stutter-invariant formulas, so its next-time operator "can void the validity of the partial order reduction algorithm" ([SPIN](https://spinroot.com/spin/Man/ltl.html)), and Alloy 6, which has no implicit stuttering, needs an explicit do-nothing event in each model ([Alloy 6 book](https://haslab.github.io/formal-software-design/overview/index.html)).

**Hiding plus a weak relation** is the process-algebra answer. CSP turns hidden events into τ, and mCRL2 offers branching and weak bisimilarity with and without divergence preservation ([FDR manual](https://dl.cocotec.io/fdr/fdr-manual.pdf); [ltscompare](https://www.mcrl2.org/web/user_manual/tools/release/ltscompare.html)). Here **the treatment of divergence is a fairness assumption in disguise**. Failures-divergences refinement counts any reachable τ-loop as a failure. Branching bisimilarity "abstracts to a large extent from divergence", which suits settings where fairness applies, but "a behavioural equivalence that abstracts from divergence is not compatible with any temporal logic featuring an eventually modality"; divergence-preserving branching bisimilarity is the coarsest equivalence that is a congruence for parallel composition and only equates processes satisfying the same CTL*−X formulas ([van Glabbeek, Luttik, Spaninks](https://arxiv.org/abs/1801.01180)). The stakes are practical. Verum moved its Dezyne tool from FDR to mCRL2 in 2018 because "FDR cannot handle fairness. Using FDR for functional verification results in many livelocks which hides refinement issue" ([Hoogendijk](https://www.ou.nl/documents/40554/577705/Hoogendijk_DMCD2018.pdf/c303582e-7099-75de-550e-40f959658a54)).

**Proof obligations** are the third answer. Event-B requires new events to refine `skip` and convergent events to decrease a variant, which rules out the endless internal activity that pure stuttering would allow ([Hoang §5.2, §7](https://www.southampton.ac.uk/~tsh2n14/publications/chapters/eventb-dbook13.pdf)). Fjfj matches each implementation rule with a sequence of specification rules ([Fjfj §6](https://adam.chlipala.net/papers/FjfjPLDI25/FjfjPLDI25.pdf)). Armada applies Cohen–Lamport reduction with movers, generating one commutativity lemma per pair of step types; CertiKOS coarsens interleavings through a chain of machine models that shuffles and merges switch points; Perennial, Grove and Verus fire the abstract step at a linearization point that the proof picks through ghost resources, even during crash recovery ([Armada §4.2](https://www.andrew.cmu.edu/user/bparno/papers/armada.pdf); [CertiKOS §3](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/conference/osdi16/osdi16-gu.pdf); [Perennial](https://pdos.csail.mit.edu/papers/perennial:sosp19.pdf)). seL4 sidesteps the question with an event-based, mostly atomic kernel on a uniprocessor ([seL4 §3](https://www.sigops.org/s/conferences/sosp/2009/papers/klein-sosp09.pdf)). Cheap syntactic disciplines buy atomic protocol steps: IronFleet requires each host step to perform all receives before all sends and at most one time-dependent operation, and Anvil allows one external request per `step()` ([IronFleet §3.6](https://www.andrew.cmu.edu/user/bparno/papers/ironfleet.pdf); [Anvil](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/osdi24-sun-xudong.pdf)). The catch is the soundness argument. IronFleet's reduction theorem stayed a paper sketch, "a machine-checked proof that this obligation enables reduction is future work" ([IronFleet §3.6](https://www.andrew.cmu.edu/user/bparno/papers/ironfleet.pdf)), whereas Armada verifies its reduction strategies once and reuses them ([Armada](https://www.andrew.cmu.edu/user/bparno/papers/armada.pdf)). The linearization point itself was chosen case by case: a quorum vote in IronRSL, recovery code in Perennial, the instant a clock read executes in Grove ([IronFleet §5.1.2](https://www.andrew.cmu.edu/user/bparno/papers/ironfleet.pdf); [Perennial §3.2](https://pdos.csail.mit.edu/papers/perennial:sosp19.pdf); [Grove §3.5](https://pdos.csail.mit.edu/papers/grove:sosp23.pdf)).

**Comparing at retirement** avoids stuttering altogether. ISA-Formal checks each instruction's architectural effect at retirement without flushing the pipeline and, unlike Burch–Dill flushing, "will detect errors caused by interactions between instructions such as errors in the forwarding logic"; out-of-order cores turned out easier, because the reorder buffer is a single point of serialization ([Reid et al.](https://alastairreid.github.io/papers/cav2016_isa_formal.pdf)). The price is a bounded horizon and extra checks that link instructions: program-counter continuity, register read-after-write, causality and unique retirement order ([riscv-formal](https://yosyshq.readthedocs.io/projects/riscv-formal/en/latest/procedure.html)).

Clock ticks are where the traditions part. **None of TLA+, Event-B, Ivy or P has a built-in clock**; TLA+ writes real time as an explicit variable ([Lamport ch. 9](https://lamport.azurewebsites.net/tla/book-02-08-08.pdf)). Bluespec gives a one-rule-at-a-time semantics and leaves cycles to a compiler-chosen schedule, so that "BSV programmers often think deliberately about static-analysis details and even change their code to nudge the compiler in the right direction" ([Kôika §1](https://people.csail.mit.edu/bthom/pldi20.pdf)). Kôika makes the schedule part of the program, gains a deterministic cycle-accurate semantics, and proves that every cycle equals some one-rule-at-a-time sequence. It also marks the limit of the interleaving view: a two-rules-per-cycle property "would be hard even to state ... because 'cycle' is not a meaningful concept in a typical ORAAT formalization" ([Kôika §4–5](https://people.csail.mit.edu/bthom/pldi20.pdf)). Model checkers took sides outright. NuSMV deprecated asynchronous processes and nuXmv removed them, while SPIN defines itself by "asynchronous control in software systems, rather than synchronous control in hardware systems" ([NuSMV](https://nusmv.fbk.eu/userman/v26/nusmv.pdf); [nuXmv](https://nuxmv.fbk.eu/downloads/nuxmv-user-manual.pdf); [Holzmann](https://spinroot.com/spin/Doc/ieee97.pdf)). Lamport warns that "a noninterleaving specification will not, in general, implement an interleaving one" ([Lamport §10.5](https://lamport.azurewebsites.net/tla/book-02-08-08.pdf)).

The considerations follow. Stuttering in the semantics makes refinement implication but limits properties to stutter-invariant ones and leaves divergence to separate liveness arguments; stuttering through a weak relation makes the fairness stance part of the relation, and Dezyne's migration shows users notice which stance they got; stuttering through obligations makes convergence explicit at the price of more of them. If cycle counts or throughput are ever part of a claim, Kôika's evidence is that the cycle has to be in the semantics and not only in the compiler, and a language with both clocked and interleaved composition needs a theorem relating them. Atomicity disciplines such as receive-then-send are cheap to check per step, but where only checked proofs count, the reduction theorem behind them has to be proved once, as Armada did and IronFleet did not.

### Relational cores under constructive surfaces keep proofs near the semantics

Steps are written in two basic styles. **Relational** notations state a predicate over the current and next state: TLA+ actions, Event-B's before-after predicates (its `:|` form is "the most general"), Alloy's guard, effect and frame predicates under `always`, NuSMV's `TRANS`, IronFleet's disjunctions of host-step predicates, Igloo's guarded event systems, and Fjfj's rules and methods as relations ([Lamport §2](https://lamport.azurewebsites.net/tla/book-02-08-08.pdf); [Hoang §5](https://www.southampton.ac.uk/~tsh2n14/publications/chapters/eventb-dbook13.pdf); [Alloy 6 book](https://haslab.github.io/formal-software-design/overview/index.html); [IronFleet §3.2](https://www.andrew.cmu.edu/user/bparno/papers/ironfleet.pdf); [Igloo](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.04749); [Fjfj §3](https://adam.chlipala.net/papers/FjfjPLDI25/FjfjPLDI25.pdf)). **Constructive** notations write a program: Ivy's imperative actions, P's event handlers, Promela's blocking guarded commands, CSP process terms inside a lazy functional language, mCRL2's ACP-style terms with data, Bluespec-style rules (as Kami formalises them) whose reads see the old state and whose writes apply together, Verdi's handlers, seL4's monadic executable specification, and Sail's first-order imperative instruction semantics ([Ivy](http://microsoft.github.io/ivy/language.html); [P](https://p-org.github.io/P/manual/statements/); [SPIN manual](https://spinroot.com/spin/Man/Manual.html); [FDR manual](https://dl.cocotec.io/fdr/fdr-manual.pdf); [mCRL2](https://www.mcrl2.org/web/user_manual/language_reference/process.html); [Kami §2](http://adam.chlipala.net/papers/KamiICFP17/KamiICFP17.pdf); [Sail](https://www.cl.cam.ac.uk/~pes20/sail/sail-popl2019.pdf)).

Relational semantics put the step predicate directly in front of the proof; constructive ones put a verification-condition generator or compiler between source and logic, and **that component joins the trusted base**. Ivy and PVerifier trust their VC generation along with Z3 or UCLID5, and Armada names its 13,191-line state-machine translator as "the only aspect of Armada we must trust" besides the Dafny toolchain ([Ivy](http://microsoft.github.io/ivy/decidability.html); [PVerifier](https://p-org.github.io/P/advanced/PVerifierLanguageExtensions/announcement); [Armada](https://www.andrew.cmu.edu/user/bparno/papers/armada.pdf)). Declarative steps can leave the implementer work the specification only describes: an IronRSL predicate defines a log-truncation point as "the nth highest number in a certain set", so someone had to write and verify code that computes it ([IronFleet §5.1.3](https://www.andrew.cmu.edu/user/bparno/papers/ironfleet.pdf)). Executable specifications run and test well but over-specify, which seL4 resolves by refining a nondeterministic abstract specification, built with `select` and `OR`, into a deterministic executable one ([seL4 §4](https://www.sigops.org/s/conferences/sosp/2009/papers/klein-sosp09.pdf)). Framing is the other hazard. Operational notations keep unassigned state unchanged for free ([NuSMV](https://nusmv.fbk.eu/userman/v26/nusmv.pdf)); declarative ones must say so, "unmentioned ≠ unchanged" being a known source of bugs ([Jackson](https://people.csail.mit.edu/dnj/talks/lipari05/lectures/lipari-lecture4.pdf)); and Event-B pays with a feasibility obligation for every nondeterministic action ([Hoang](https://www.southampton.ac.uk/~tsh2n14/publications/chapters/eventb-dbook13.pdf)).

Two details matter for hardware. A failing guard can mean "not enabled", as in Kami and Bluespec, where "assertions are guards to control which rules are enabled when", or "abort and revert", as in Kôika and Fjfj; and intra-cycle forwarding can be part of the language, as Kôika's port annotations are, or hidden in the compiler ([Kami §2](http://adam.chlipala.net/papers/KamiICFP17/KamiICFP17.pdf); [Kôika §2](https://people.csail.mit.edu/bthom/pldi20.pdf)). Kinds of nondeterminism differ as well. CSP separates internal choice from external choice resolved by the environment, and that split is what makes refusals observable and failures-based interface contracts statable; Promela, Alloy and NuSMV have a single kind of nondeterminism, and Dezyne needed an explicit `illegal` marker to say what a client must not send ([FDR manual](https://dl.cocotec.io/fdr/fdr-manual.pdf); [Dezyne](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.02962)).

The pattern that recurs is **a relational core under a constructive surface, with a discipline that catches frame errors**. Quint gives TLA+'s semantics a typed surface with delayed assignment, `all` and `any` blocks and modes, giving up theorems, hiding and parameterized instances along the way ([Quint](https://quint.sh/docs/lang)); many AWS engineers began in PlusCal, while the tools work on TLA+ ([Newcombe et al.](https://lamport.azurewebsites.net/tla/formal-methods-amazon.pdf)); Armada mixes imperative levels with declarative `somehow` statements ([Armada](https://www.andrew.cmu.edu/user/bparno/papers/armada.pdf)); Verus separates `spec`, `proof` and `exec` code and erases the ghost parts ([Verus 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.05491)); mCRL2 linearises every model into one data-parameterised guarded-command form before analysis ([Groote & de Vink](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14956)). ISA specifications gain reuse by hiding state access behind primitives. Sail's prover output is a free monad over effect requests whose meaning a concurrency model supplies, and Sail's RISC-V model, adopted by RISC-V International, generates a C emulator, Isabelle and Rocq definitions and an experimental Lean output from one text ([Sail §4.2](https://www.cl.cam.ac.uk/~pes20/sail/sail-popl2019.pdf); [sail-riscv](https://github.com/riscv/sail-riscv)). The open question for a kernel-checked language is where the surface-to-core translation sits in the trust story: as a trusted elaborator in the manner of Ivy, P and Armada, or as a producer of steps the kernel checks. Tool drift is a further warning, since TLC departs from TLA+ semantics for `CHOOSE` and strings "for reasons of efficiency" ([Lamport §14.6](https://lamport.azurewebsites.net/tla/book-02-08-08.pdf)).

### Specifications need unbounded data, circuits need bits, and the seam costs most

The type disciplines run from untyped set theory to fixed-width bits. TLA+ is untyped, type correctness being "just a name for a certain invariance property", and its symbolic tools each added a type system, from Apalache's annotations to Quint ([Lamport §3](https://lamport.azurewebsites.net/tla/book-02-08-08.pdf); [Apalache](https://apalache-mc.org/docs/apalache/features.html); [Quint](https://quint.sh/docs/lang)). Event-B is typed set theory with inferred types and carrier sets that serve as parameters, which gave its provers and SMT translation typed input from the start ([Hoang §2, §4](https://www.southampton.ac.uk/~tsh2n14/publications/chapters/eventb-dbook13.pdf)). Ivy keeps sorts uninterpreted and admits arithmetic only in decidability-preserving ways ([Ivy](http://microsoft.github.io/ivy/decidability.html)). mCRL2 has unbounded algebraic data defined by rewrite equations, with "no largest number" in its numeric sorts ([mCRL2 data](https://www.mcrl2.org/web/user_manual/language_reference/data.html)). FDR and SPIN require finite state, and the cost is visible: `channel c : Int` makes FDR explore 2^32 branches at every input ([FDR manual §6.1.2](https://dl.cocotec.io/fdr/fdr-manual.pdf)). Alloy bounds everything by scope and integer bit-width, and its wrap-around integers produced spurious counterexamples until the semantics was repaired ([Milicevic & Jackson](https://people.csail.mit.edu/aleks/website/papers/abz12-overflow.pdf)). Hardware implementations are bit-vectors with records and arrays, and ISA specifications pair unbounded integers with width-indexed bit-vectors, Sail checking width constraints with Z3 inside its type checker ([Sail](https://www.cl.cam.ac.uk/~pes20/sail/sail-popl2019.pdf)).

**Data design and state-space design are one decision.** Unbounded data keeps models faithful and pushes the work to symbolic or deductive methods; mCRL2's symbolic solvers have handled equation systems with "well over 10^50" variables ([Groote & de Vink](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14956)). Finite types give push-button checking but need meta-theory such as data independence or type reduction to say anything general ([Mazur & Lowe](https://arxiv.org/abs/1201.1716)). Bounded scopes give quick answers with no generality. When the analyser's encoding drives the data language, users hit walls, as AWS did with Alloy ([Newcombe et al.](https://lamport.azurewebsites.net/tla/formal-methods-amazon.pdf)). For bit-accurate RTL, finite bit-vectors are faithful rather than approximate, so the finite stance costs little there and much more at the system level of queues, counters and node identifiers.

Every proof-based framework mixes the two worlds in one language by convention. Kami admits any Coq type in specification-only registers, whose designs "cannot be synthesized to circuits", and swaps a bit-level queue for one holding a Coq list ([Kami §2–3](http://adam.chlipala.net/papers/KamiICFP17/KamiICFP17.pdf)); Fjfj represents primitive modules by nondeterministic relations used "heavily ... in specifications but not synthesizable implementations" ([Fjfj §3.2](https://adam.chlipala.net/papers/FjfjPLDI25/FjfjPLDI25.pdf)); Verus gives specifications `int` and `nat` while executable code uses machine integers ([Verus 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.05491)). **The line between implementable and specification-only data is a convention, not a checked boundary.** Data refinement uses a handful of devices: refinement mappings (TLA+); gluing invariants with witnesses that carry their own feasibility obligation (Event-B); module substitution (Kami, and P, where it is tested); abstraction functions, which IronFleet preferred because "the proof is easier with a function", and Verus's `View`; ghost-variable introduction and hiding (Armada); or a proof that an unbounded version equals a bounded one, as for an mCRL2 sliding-window protocol verified first with unbounded and then with modulo sequence numbers ([Hoang §7](https://www.southampton.ac.uk/~tsh2n14/publications/chapters/eventb-dbook13.pdf); [IronFleet §3.3](https://www.andrew.cmu.edu/user/bparno/papers/ironfleet.pdf); [Armada §4.2](https://www.andrew.cmu.edu/user/bparno/papers/armada.pdf); [Groote & de Vink](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14956)).

Encoding boundaries cost the most per line. IronFleet's hardest implementation proof was that what one host marshals into bytes another parses back; a protocol-specific library took **a person-month**, a generic grammar-based one several more weeks, after which IronRSL's specific parts took two hours ([IronFleet §3.5, §5.3](https://www.andrew.cmu.edu/user/bparno/papers/ironfleet.pdf)). Verdi in 2015 modelled packets as abstract datatypes and left serialization outside the proof ([Verdi §3](https://homes.cs.washington.edu/~mernst/pubs/verify-distsystem-pldi2015.pdf)). Hardware meets the same boundary at bit-level encodings and adds width polymorphism: Arm found removing ASL's bit-width polymorphism "the most challenging problem" in translating its specification to Verilog, and Kami's authors had to add about 7,000 lines of bit-vector and related library to Coq ([Reid et al. §4](https://alastairreid.github.io/papers/cav2016_isa_formal.pdf); [Kami §6.5](http://adam.chlipala.net/papers/KamiICFP17/KamiICFP17.pdf)). Undefined and arbitrary values are a soundness trap across layers. FIRRTL models `DontCare` and similar results as free values because "if we first generate Verilog and then use yosys, we are only verifying one concrete translation of the design" ([Laeufer et al.](https://woset-workshop.github.io/PDFs/2021/a03.pdf)); Lightbulb reconciled hardware without undefined behaviour and software with it through a simulation relation and a "simple but strenuous" proof ([Erbsen et al. §5.8](https://adam.chlipala.net/papers/LightbulbPLDI21/LightbulbPLDI21.pdf)); Armada relies on "the careful attention of the specification writer" to keep undefined behaviour out of the specification ([Armada §3.2.3](https://www.andrew.cmu.edu/user/bparno/papers/armada.pdf)). Verus keeps integer and bit-vector reasoning in separate modes, and its EPR mode abstracts keys to a total order to make a protocol invariant decidable ([Verus 2024](https://www.andrew.cmu.edu/user/bparno/papers/verus-sys.pdf)).

The questions left open are whether the core is typed and who infers types; whether the implementable versus specification-only boundary is checked or conventional; whether arbitrary and undefined values are free choices or fixed by one lowering; whether bit-width is a parameter of definitions or monomorphised away; and whether bit-level, word-level and integer reasoning are separate modes with explicit bridges.

### Compositional refinement needs a precongruence, a well-founded assumption and narrow interfaces

Compositional refinement works only when the refinement relation is preserved by the composition and hiding operators in use. CSP has this for every operator: "if P ⊑ Q, then for any CSP context C, C[P] ⊑ C[Q]" ([FDR manual §6.1](https://dl.cocotec.io/fdr/fdr-manual.pdf)). Dezyne builds its industrial method on it, checking each component against its interfaces after hiding and composing the results because "the Failures Refinement relation is transitive and the hiding operator is congruent" ([Dezyne](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.02962)). The weak equivalences of CCS- and ACP-style algebras lack it: divergence-preserving branching bisimilarity "is not a congruence for any language that includes choice" and needs a root condition for choice and for recursion ([van Glabbeek et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1801.01180)). The payoff of a congruence is large in practice. FDR compresses components before composing them, and on Dezyne models a monolithic mCRL2 run was **2–5 times slower** than FDR, with a further 5–10 times gained by mimicking FDR's compositional approach by hand ([van Beusekom et al.](https://www.win.tue.nl/~timw/downloads/Formalising_the_Dezyne_Modelling_Language_in_mCRL2.pdf)).

Circular assume-guarantee needs a well-founded argument. Ivy and P's ModP both let components assume each other's guarantees, and both are sound because the assumption covers only the past, "we are only assuming that the other assertion has always held _in the past_", which is induction on trace length ([Ivy](http://microsoft.github.io/ivy/language.html); [ModP](https://ankushdesai.github.io/assets/papers/modp.pdf)). ModP proves compositional safety and circular assume-guarantee theorems for systems whose topology changes at runtime, then discharges them by testing ([ModP](https://ankushdesai.github.io/assets/papers/modp.pdf)). CertiKOS composes per-CPU proofs through rely conditions and defines parallel composition over disjoint thread sets as intersection of behaviours ([CertiKOS](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/conference/osdi16/osdi16-gu.pdf)). Igloo proves that the traces of a parallel composition are the composition of the components' traces, so refining each component refines the system, and lets components be verified in different languages with different verifiers ([Igloo](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.04749)).

**Interface concurrency decides modular cost**, and hardware supplies the cleanest evidence. Kami proved substitution, replication (`a ⊑ b` gives `n·a ⊑ n·b`) and inlining theorems for trace refinement over method labels, with each method callable at most once per step "because method calls are translated into wires in hardware" ([Kami §3.2, §5](http://adam.chlipala.net/papers/KamiICFP17/KamiICFP17.pdf)). Its labels admit arbitrary sets of simultaneous calls, however, and "the result was a number of proof cases that grew exponentially in the size of the module's API" ([Fjfj](https://adam.chlipala.net/papers/FjfjPLDI25/FjfjPLDI25.pdf)). Fjfj allows at most one state-changing method per submodule per rule, which restores per-method specifications "as we are used to from e.g. frameworks for verifying object-oriented programs"; a scan of **about 100,000 lines of Bluespec** across five designs found the restriction "already quite commonly followed" ([Fjfj §2, §4, §6.2](https://adam.chlipala.net/papers/FjfjPLDI25/FjfjPLDI25.pdf)). Fjfj also warns that inlining can hide the once-per-cycle restriction "and allow illegal (physically impossible) executions to proceed" ([Fjfj §2](https://adam.chlipala.net/papers/FjfjPLDI25/FjfjPLDI25.pdf)). The same lever appears in distributed systems as IronFleet's receive-then-send step and Anvil's one request per step. Modular proofs also keep needing specifications **more nondeterministic than the natural one**, followed by a separate proof that the generalisation is harmless in context. Fjfj's processor specification gains speculative "load machines" and a second refinement shows `FullSystem(GeneralizedProcessorSpec, Memory) ⊑ FullSystem(SimpleProcessorSpec, Memory)`; Kami needed memory adapters and a label map hiding a cache's `peek` method; IronRSL refines a machine that advances when a quorum votes rather than when a replica executes ([Fjfj §8](https://adam.chlipala.net/papers/FjfjPLDI25/FjfjPLDI25.pdf); [Kami §5–6](http://adam.chlipala.net/papers/KamiICFP17/KamiICFP17.pdf); [IronFleet §5.1.2](https://www.andrew.cmu.edu/user/bparno/papers/ironfleet.pdf)).

For distributed protocols the environment is a component too, and the frameworks state it as explicit semantics. Verdi adds rules for duplication, dropping, timeouts, crashes and reboots to its network semantics, and its transformers move a system from one fault model to another; it has no Byzantine semantics ([Verdi §2–3](https://homes.cs.washington.edu/~mernst/pubs/verify-distsystem-pldi2015.pdf)). IronFleet trusts a UDP specification under which packets may be delayed, dropped or duplicated but not tampered with ([IronFleet](https://www.andrew.cmu.edu/user/bparno/papers/ironfleet.pdf)); Grove's network may reorder, drop and duplicate, nodes crash losing their heap but keeping durable state, and a clock returns bounds on global time ([Grove §3.1](https://pdos.csail.mit.edu/papers/grove:sosp23.pdf)); Igloo decomposes a model into components and an environment that can include fault or attacker models ([Igloo](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.04749)); seL4 leaves user transitions fully nondeterministic, covering behaviours "benign, buggy, or malicious" ([seL4 §4.5](https://www.sigops.org/s/conferences/sosp/2009/papers/klein-sosp09.pdf)). Liveness then needs an assumption about the environment, such as Anvil's that faults eventually stop ([Anvil](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/osdi24-sun-xudong.pdf)).

Not every project found composition worth its cost. Lamport advises that a specification written from scratch is "probably better" monolithic ([Lamport §10.9](https://lamport.azurewebsites.net/tla/book-02-08-08.pdf)); Perennial's Mailboat proof uses one abstraction relation where the earlier CMAIL proof needed 11 intermediate interfaces and five abstraction relations ([Perennial](https://pdos.csail.mit.edu/papers/perennial:sosp19.pdf)); Grove argues that state machines make larger systems "challenging to compose" and composes through separation-logic specifications instead ([Grove](https://pdos.csail.mit.edu/papers/grove:sosp23.pdf)); IronFleet's own authors judged that Verdi's transformers offer "a cleaner approach to composition" than their fixed three-layer stack ([IronFleet §9.3](https://www.andrew.cmu.edu/user/bparno/papers/ironfleet.pdf)). Industrial hardware flows mostly flatten, as Chisel's btor2 path does by inlining everything into one module, or state assume-guarantee contracts at the IR level, as CIRCT does ([Laeufer et al.](https://woset-workshop.github.io/PDFs/2021/a03.pdf); [CIRCT](https://circt.llvm.org/docs/Dialects/Verif/)); no source showed any tool checking that mutually assumed interface specifications compose soundly.

Synchronous and asynchronous composition both have to exist somewhere. CSP synchronises any number of processes on shared alphabets, which fits shared clocks and buses; mCRL2's `a || b` equals `a.b + b.a + a|b`, so simultaneous multi-actions sit beside interleaving, with explicit `comm` and `allow` operators whose order matters; Promela offers binary rendezvous and buffered channels for software ([FDR manual](https://dl.cocotec.io/fdr/fdr-manual.pdf); [mCRL2](https://www.mcrl2.org/web/user_manual/language_reference/process.html); [SPIN manual](https://spinroot.com/spin/Man/Manual.html)). The open questions are whether the refinement relation is a precongruence for every operator the language offers, and what observables that rules in or out; whether interfaces admit simultaneous interactions, and at what proof cost; what well-founded argument the logic states for circular assumptions; how clocked and interleaved composition relate; how fault and attacker environments are composed in; and whether the generalise-then-justify step gets first-class support.

The options on each axis, side by side, as the evidence in this section records them:

## Parameterized and cross-layer claims still live in separate worlds

### Every for-all-N result rests on an inductive invariant someone had to invent

Model checkers verify fixed instances. TLC and Apalache need constants fixed, the P checker tests configurations, FDR, SPIN and mCRL2 explore finite state spaces, and Alloy checks within a scope ([Apalache](https://apalache-mc.org/docs/apalache/features.html); [P tests](https://p-org.github.io/P/manual/testcases/); [Alloy 6 book](https://haslab.github.io/formal-software-design/overview/index.html)). IronFleet's authors sum up that model checking "requires that the system be instantiated with finite, typically tiny, parameters" ([IronFleet §9.2](https://www.andrew.cmu.edu/user/bparno/papers/ironfleet.pdf)), and Kami's survey of coherence work records explicit-state tools handling "only tens of addresses and CPUs" ([Kami §8](http://adam.chlipala.net/papers/KamiICFP17/KamiICFP17.pdf)). **The parameterized model-checking problem is undecidable in general** ([Mazur & Lowe](https://arxiv.org/abs/1201.1716)), so generality comes from three sources. **Meta-theorems** reduce an infinite family to finitely many instances for restricted classes: cutoffs ([Bloem et al.](https://finkbeiner.groups.cispa.de/publications/BJKKRVW15.html)), threshold automata for Byzantine threshold-guarded algorithms ([Konnov et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.14789)), and CSP's data independence and type reduction ([Mazur & Lowe](https://arxiv.org/abs/1201.1716)); symmetry reduction shrinks an instance but does not generalise it ([TopSPIN](https://www.doc.ic.ac.uk/~afd/topspin)). **Deductive proof over quantified inductive invariants** is how TLAPS, Ivy, Event-B, PVerifier and every verified-systems framework obtain results for a symbolic number of nodes ([TLAPS](https://arxiv.org/abs/1208.5933); [PVerifier](https://p-org.github.io/P/advanced/PVerifierLanguageExtensions/announcement); [Igloo](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.04749)), how Kami proved its multicore "for arbitrary core counts and arbitrary sizes of (direct-mapped) caches and cache lines" ([Kami §7.1](http://adam.chlipala.net/papers/KamiICFP17/KamiICFP17.pdf)), and how HOL-CSP moved CSP refinement into Isabelle to prove the dining philosophers for any N ([Taha, Wolff, Ye](https://www.lri.fr/~wolff/papers/conf/2020-iFM_CSP.pdf)). **Invariant inference** learns candidates from small instances and checks them for every size.

The central design choice is whether verification conditions stay decidable. Ivy keeps them in EPR, so Z3 always answers and gives finite counter-models, and Paxos Made EPR verified Multi-, Vertical, Fast and Stoppable Paxos that way, the last three for the first time; the cost is that cycles of quantifier alternation must be broken by hand with derived relations ([Ivy](http://microsoft.github.io/ivy/decidability.html); [Padon et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.07191)). TLA+, Event-B and P accept undecidable conditions and rely on interactive proof or SMT heuristics. Verus shows the trade-off can be made locally: moving one delegation-map operation into EPR mode replaced about 300 lines and several days of proof with automatic checking, while a distributed-lock example took about 100 lines of EPR boilerplate against 25 in default mode ([Verus 2024](https://www.andrew.cmu.edu/user/bparno/papers/verus-sys.pdf)).

Inference has advanced quickly. I4 generalises an invariant found on a small instance; IC3PO infers quantifier structure from symmetry and finds cutoffs automatically; DistAI learns universally quantified invariants; DuoAI handles existential ones and solves Paxos more than two orders of magnitude faster than earlier methods; Basilisk derives "provenance invariants" by static analysis and proved safety for all 16 protocols it tried, including Multi-Paxos ([I4](https://web.eecs.umich.edu/~manosk/assets/papers/i4-sosp19.pdf); [IC3PO](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.14831); [DistAI](https://www.cs.columbia.edu/~nieh/pubs/osdi2021_distai.pdf); [DuoAI](https://www.usenix.org/conference/osdi22/presentation/yao); [Basilisk](https://www.usenix.org/conference/osdi25/presentation/zhang-tony)). Kondo has the developer prove a synchronous protocol and generates the asynchronous version with its regular invariants ([Kondo](https://www.usenix.org/conference/osdi24/presentation/zhang-nuda)). The May 2026 preprint IC3Syn puts an LLM inside an IC3 controller over TLA+ states and reports invariants for **all 29** benchmark protocols, against 26 for IC3PO, 24 of 28 for Endive, 17 for SWISS and 23 for the same LLM without IC3, with inductiveness for the unbounded protocol proved in TLAPS ([Cao et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.24619v1)). Its claims need care: the suite comes mostly from one earlier collection, runs were on one machine without variance reported, and the paper calls its outputs both "candidate" and "proven". Liveness lags behind: LVR synthesises ranking functions and proves several protocols live "mostly automatically" ([Yao et al.](https://www.cs.columbia.edu/~rgu/publications/popl24-yao.pdf)).

The failure points recur across papers: ordering and arithmetic, universally-quantified-only invariant shapes, quantifier-alternation cycles broken by hand, and expert-written templates ([Cao et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.24619v1); [Padon et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.07191)), plus liveness and Byzantine protocols outside the threshold-automata fragment ([Konnov et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.14789)). Crash-fault and Byzantine protocols live in separate formalisms, and **no tool was found that handles both in one logic with one inference engine**. Invariants dominate manual cost wherever they are hand-written: **80% of the effort** in seL4's first refinement went into invariants, and Verdi's Raft proof required "iteratively discovering and proving 90 system invariants" ([seL4 §5.2](https://www.sigops.org/s/conferences/sosp/2009/papers/klein-sosp09.pdf); [Verdi-Raft](https://homes.cs.washington.edu/~mernst/pubs/raft-proof-cpp2016.pdf)). Two consequences follow for a proof-producing toolchain. A result checked for N ≤ k and one that holds for all N are different artefacts, and only the second is a parameterized result. And any meta-theorem used to bridge them, whether a cutoff, data independence or type reduction, has to be a checked theorem itself, proved once and instantiated, or the every-N claim rests on an unchecked argument.

### No machine-checked stack yet runs from a decentralized protocol down to RTL

Machine-checked hardware-to-software theorems exist, but only inside one higher-order proof assistant and only for small systems. **Lightbulb** joins an application, device drivers, a compiler and a pipelined RISC-V processor in one Coq theorem stated against the application spec and the lowest hardware model. Its trust "bottoms out" at the automatic Kami-to-Bluespec translation, with the Bluespec compiler, Yosys and nextpnr, a 200-line Verilog wrapper and about 400 lines of Kami semantics in the trusted base. It took **about four person-years**, ran about ten times slower than the unverified prototype, and its authors attribute most of the cost to "accidental complexity caused by tooling issues", including SMT bridges to Coq that "crashed or ran too slowly" ([Erbsen et al.](https://adam.chlipala.net/papers/LightbulbPLDI21/LightbulbPLDI21.pdf)). GarageDoor claims a whole-system Coq proof with no compiler or verification tool trusted beyond Coq's kernel, though whether its processor is verified was not established ([GarageDoor](https://adam.chlipala.net/papers/GarageDoorPLDI24/)). Kôika has a verified compiler to a minimal RTL with only a "thin unverified" printer to Verilog ([Kôika §6.5](https://people.csail.mit.edu/bthom/pldi20.pdf)), and supported a Coq proof of timing isolation on a multicore pipelined RISC-V design ([Lau et al.](https://adam.chlipala.net/papers/IsolationCCS24/)). CakeML on Silver composes compiler and processor theorems in HOL4 with a proof-producing Verilog generator, and Lööw's Lutsig is a verified synthesis tool for a class of FPGAs ([Lööw et al.](https://trustworthy.systems/publications/full_text/Loow_KTMNAF_19.pdf); [Lööw](https://www.doc.ic.ac.uk/~aloow/papers/thesis.pdf)). Knox and Parfait relate the wire-level behaviour of hardware security modules to functional specs through information-preserving refinement, Parfait reaching an ECDSA signing module ([Knox](https://www.usenix.org/conference/osdi22/presentation/athalye); [Parfait](https://people.csail.mit.edu/nickolai/papers/athalye-parfait.pdf)). Google's Silver Oak built an AES block in a Coq-embedded HDL that was a drop-in replacement for OpenTitan's, but its compiler to SystemVerilog is unverified ([Silver Oak](https://github.com/project-oak/silveroak)).

The closest thing to protocol and hardware in one refinement framework is Kami's coherence work. Because guarded atomic rules have the same shape as guarded-command protocol specifications, Kami verified a directory-based MSI coherence protocol in the same language as its pipeline and proved `n·P4st + n·MRqRsEx + Mcache⁽ⁿ⁾ ⊑ SC⁽ⁿ⁾` for any n ([Kami §6](http://adam.chlipala.net/papers/KamiICFP17/KamiICFP17.pdf)). That protocol is a hardware protocol with no message loss, crashes or Byzantine faults, and its proof needed 6 main and 30 low-level mutually inductive invariants, discharged largely by hand because "it took less time to discharge individual goals manually than wait for the proof automation to complete" ([Kami §6.3, §6.5](http://adam.chlipala.net/papers/KamiICFP17/KamiICFP17.pdf)). Industrial cross-layer checks are bounded. ISA-Formal model-checks instruction sequences against Arm's specification, and RTLCheck generates per-litmus-test assertions for JasperGold, verifying a multicore RISC-V design sequentially consistent across 56 litmus tests after a bug fix ([Reid et al.](https://alastairreid.github.io/papers/cav2016_isa_formal.pdf); [RTLCheck](https://research.nvidia.com/publication/2017-10_rtlcheck-verifying-memory-consistency-rtl-designs)). Protocol-to-implementation stacks such as IronFleet and Verdi stop at the operating system and network, and an empirical study found **16 bugs in them, mostly in the shims** between verified and unverified code and none in the verified protocols ([Fonseca et al.](https://www.cs.purdue.edu/homes/pfonseca/papers/eurosys2017-dsbugs.pdf)).

At the specification level one notation already spans both worlds: the 2026 TLAPS-Bench includes TLA+ specifications of cache coherence and of Byzantine consensus ([TLAPS-Bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02849)). But **no stack was found that connects a parameterized, asynchronous, fault-tolerant protocol, by machine-checked refinement, to an N-instance RTL implementation**, and no work links a cutoff argument, which derives quantified invariants from finite instances, to an RTL instance that is itself finite. This is absence of evidence across the sources searched, and industrial ACL2 work, which the research did not examine, could qualify it. The existing stacks suggest two considerations for a new language. The central trust decision is where the checked artefact sits relative to silicon (a high-level HDL model, emitted RTL, or a netlist); Kôika narrowed the unverified gap to a printer plus synthesis. And the people who built Lightbulb locate its cost in tooling rather than inherent difficulty, which, if it generalises beyond one project, leaves room for automation designed into a language rather than emulated through tactics.

## Checkable evidence exists only at the bottom of the stack

### Certificates cover SAT and bit-level safety, not theories, quantifiers or liveness

A small kernel helps only if every tool above it produces something the kernel can check. The de Bruijn criterion asks for proofs checkable by a small independent program; a survey of 27 provers found HOL Light's kernel to be only several hundred lines of OCaml and most other provers without such a kernel ([Paulson](https://lawrencecpaulson.github.io/2022/01/05/LCF.html); [Survey](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.03028)), and Candle's soundness theorem reaches the machine code that runs ([Candle](https://cakeml.org/candle/)). Across the surveyed systems, however, **evidence is asymmetric: failures come with counterexamples anyone can replay, passes come with nothing**. FDR gives counterexamples and a debugger but nothing for a passed refinement; mCRL2 emits evidence models for μ-calculus results and counterexamples for three equivalences; Alloy's Kodkod returns unsatisfiable cores, which are diagnostics; and P's checker gives a "reproducible error-trace", evidence of bugs rather than of their absence ([FDR manual](https://dl.cocotec.io/fdr/fdr-manual.pdf); [pbessolve](https://www.mcrl2.org/web/user_manual/tools/release/pbessolve.html); [ltscompare](https://www.mcrl2.org/web/user_manual/tools/release/ltscompare.html); [Kodkod](https://emina.github.io/kodkod/); [P](https://p-org.github.io/P/whatisP/)). TLAPS is the protocol world's closest approach to a small-kernel design: Zenon emits Isar proofs that Isabelle checks, so "Zenon is therefore not part of the trusted code base", but the SMT and LS4 backends are trusted, and release 1.4.5 "fixes a soundness bug" in 1.4.4 ([Chaudhuri et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1011.2560); [tlapm releases](https://github.com/tlaplus/tlapm/releases)). Rodin trusts external provers as oracles, and its SMT plug-in, when a solver lacks unsatisfiable cores, "will assert that the full Event-B sequent is valid" ([Déharbe et al.](https://members.loria.fr/PFontaine//Deharbe8.pdf)). SMT-based systems frameworks trust their verifier, VC generation and Z3, and Verus also trusts idiom automation whose result "the SMT encoding then simply assumes" ([Verus 2024 §3.3](https://www.andrew.cmu.edu/user/bparno/papers/verus-sys.pdf)); none of IronFleet, Armada, Verus or Anvil reports an independently re-checkable proof object. Two alternatives to trusting a checker exist. A verified checker such as CAVA, an LTL model checker proved in Isabelle, is slower than unverified ones, and its authors judge "verifying functional correctness of significant parts of SPIN is not a practical proposition today" ([Esparza et al.](https://www.proof.cit.tum.de/~nipkow/pubs/cav13.pdf)); a certifying checker, in Namjoshi's sense, emits a "certificate of goodness" checkable "by simple, non-fixpoint methods" ([Namjoshi](https://link.springer.com/doi/10.1007/3-540-44585-4_2)).

Certification has matured from the bottom up. SAT competitions have required DRAT certificates for almost a decade, and cake_lpr checks LRAT proofs with a correctness theorem that reaches x64 machine code ([Froleyks et al.](https://cca.informatik.uni-freiburg.de/papers/FroleyksYuPreinerBiereHeljanko-CAV25.pdf); [Tan, Heule, Myreen](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7984575/)). Bit-level hardware model checking followed in 2024, when HWMCC made **witness circuits mandatory**: a safe result needs an AIGER circuit that simulates the model and has an inductive strengthened property, checked by five SAT queries and a polynomial reset check. In the competition **44 of 1,536 certificates were invalid**, every model checker produced invalid certificates under the organisers' fuzzing beforehand, validation added only 34% to the winner's total time, over 80% of witnesses were under twice the model's size, and the certifying winner rIC3 beat the non-certifying 2020 winner ABC ([Froleyks et al.](https://cca.informatik.uni-freiburg.de/papers/FroleyksYuPreinerBiereHeljanko-CAV25.pdf)). SMT is partly covered: cvc5's Alethe proofs can be reconstructed in Isabelle and Coq, and its native format is checked by Ethos, but rules not yet formalised are emitted as trust steps, and Ethos then reports "incomplete" rather than "correct" ([cvc5 Alethe](https://cvc5.github.io/docs/latest/proofs/output_alethe.html); [cvc5 CPC](https://cvc5.github.io/docs/latest/proofs/output_cpc.html)). **Word-level model checking still requires only counterexamples**, the 2025 liveness track's certification is undocumented, and the paper that introduced the certificates names word-level certificates, liveness certificates and a fully verified certificate checker as open problems ([HWMCC 2025](https://hwmcc.github.io/2025); [Froleyks et al.](https://cca.informatik.uni-freiburg.de/papers/FroleyksYuPreinerBiereHeljanko-CAV25.pdf)).

Residues of trust remain even at the bottom. "Certified" often means checked by another unverified solver: Certifaiger answers its SAT queries with Kissat, and the usual SAT workflow elaborates DRAT to LRAT with an unverified tool before the verified check ([Certifaiger](https://github.com/Froleyks/certifaiger); [Tan, Heule, Myreen](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7984575/)). Speed can buy trust back: Lean's `bv_decide` checks LRAT with a verified checker but uses reflection that "adds the Lean compiler to the trusted code base" ([Lean](https://lean-lang.org/doc/api/Lean/Elab/Tactic/BVDecide.html)). Specifications are the largest residue. Trusted top-level specs can be small (85 and 34 lines for IronFleet's two systems, 49 lines for Grove's key-value store) but they have bugs: Lightbulb found RISC-V specification bugs only by proving two independently written specs equivalent, and Arm ran its ASL specification against a conformance suite "with billions of instructions" before trusting it ([IronFleet](https://www.andrew.cmu.edu/user/bparno/papers/ironfleet.pdf); [Grove](https://pdos.csail.mit.edu/papers/grove:sosp23.pdf); [Erbsen et al. §5.5](https://adam.chlipala.net/papers/LightbulbPLDI21/LightbulbPLDI21.pdf); [Reid et al.](https://alastairreid.github.io/papers/cav2016_isa_formal.pdf)).

The certifying-checker literature indicates what each kind of positive result would need: an inductive invariant or witness circuit for safety, a ranking function or ranking circuit for liveness, proof annotations for μ-calculus results, the relation itself for simulation or bisimulation, and a trace for non-equivalence ([Namjoshi](https://link.springer.com/doi/10.1007/3-540-44585-4_2); [Certifaiger](https://github.com/Froleyks/certifaiger)). Refinement in the failures models against a nondeterministic specification is harder, because the check normalises the specification first, and a certificate built on the normal form could be exponential; that is an inference from FDR3's description, not a measured result ([FDR3](https://www.cs.ox.ac.uk/files/6237/Document.pdf)). **No published measurement was found of how large certificates for every-N proofs would be or how fast a kernel could check them**, which leaves the cost of certifying parameterized protocols an open quantity.

### Model checkers find bugs, proof assistants carry composition, invariants stay manual

Engines and languages are coupled. FDR's refinement check normalises the specification first and relies on specifications being "relatively small"; partial-order reduction needs stutter-invariant properties or independence information the language exposes; SAT-based bounded analysis needs bounded relational encodings; constructs that fit none of these are left to proof ([FDR3](https://www.cs.ox.ac.uk/files/6237/Document.pdf); [SPIN](https://spinroot.com/spin/Man/ltl.html); [Kodkod](https://emina.github.io/kodkod/)). Within those limits the engines are strong. FDR3 has verified systems with **over ten billion states** in a few hours, mCRL2 reduces explicit state spaces of around 10^10 states, AWS found DynamoDB bugs needing 35-step traces, and Arm found a five-instruction forwarding bug with about a dozen properties in two minutes ([FDR3](https://www.cs.ox.ac.uk/files/6237/Document.pdf); [Groote & de Vink](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14956); [Newcombe et al.](https://lamport.azurewebsites.net/tla/formal-methods-amazon.pdf); [Reid et al.](https://alastairreid.github.io/papers/cav2016_isa_formal.pdf)). Alloy 6 pairs bounded SAT analysis with complete checking through NuSMV or nuXmv on the same specification ([Alloy 6](https://alloytools.org/alloy6.html)), and adding SMT solvers to Rodin's provers cut the sequents needing interactive proof to about a quarter ([Déharbe et al.](https://members.loria.fr/PFontaine//Deharbe8.pdf)).

Proof-based work buys parametric and compositional results at a price that runs **from about 3 to over 90 lines of proof per line of code**. Verus reports 5.1 to 1 across its case studies and 3–61 times faster verification than prior tools; IronFleet 3.6 to 1 at its implementation layer and 3.7 person-years overall; Grove 12 to 1; seL4 about 200,000 lines of Isabelle and about 20 person-years for 8,700 lines of C; Verdi's Raft 50,000 lines of proof for 530 lines of code ([Verus 2024](https://www.andrew.cmu.edu/user/bparno/papers/verus-sys.pdf); [IronFleet](https://www.andrew.cmu.edu/user/bparno/papers/ironfleet.pdf); [Grove](https://pdos.csail.mit.edu/papers/grove:sosp23.pdf); [seL4](https://www.sigops.org/s/conferences/sosp/2009/papers/klein-sosp09.pdf); [Verdi-Raft](https://homes.cs.washington.edu/~mernst/pubs/raft-proof-cpp2016.pdf)). In hardware, Fjfj's crossbar, processor and network switch took 0.5, 3 and 9 person-months, with proofs roughly 14 to 24 times the implementation's size, computed from its table ([Fjfj Table 2](https://adam.chlipala.net/papers/FjfjPLDI25/FjfjPLDI25.pdf)). Liveness accounts for roughly 30% of IronFleet's proof lines, computed from its own table ([IronFleet Fig. 12](https://www.andrew.cmu.edu/user/bparno/papers/ironfleet.pdf)). Armada shows a different distribution of effort, generating about 50,000 lines of proof from about 260 hand-written lines for a barrier ([Armada §6](https://www.andrew.cmu.edu/user/bparno/papers/armada.pdf)). Maintenance is a cost of its own: an API change touching under 5% of seL4's code took about a person-year, 17% of the original proof effort, and Verdi-Raft's authors name proof maintenance their primary challenge ([seL4 §5.2](https://www.sigops.org/s/conferences/sosp/2009/papers/klein-sosp09.pdf); [Verdi-Raft](https://homes.cs.washington.edu/~mernst/pubs/raft-proof-cpp2016.pdf)). These ratios are not directly comparable, since Verus counts solver-discharged obligations as zero lines and Armada counts generated proof separately.

Industrial practice tests the gap between specification and shipped code rather than proving it. **Design-to-code conformance is AWS's recurring pain point over a decade**, now addressed by checking production logs against P specifications with PObserve ([PObserve](https://p-org.github.io/P/advanced/pobserve/pobserve)); TLA+ trace validation found discrepancies between specification and implementation in every case studied ([Cirstea et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.16075)); MongoDB found trace-checking against a highly abstract specification impractical but model-based test generation highly successful ([Davis et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.00915)); Ivy's QUIC model drove testing that found a Heartbleed-like leak and denial-of-service vulnerabilities, though an independent replication found it needs "a deep understanding of the Ivy language, the QUIC protocol, and the target implementation" ([McMillan & Zuck](https://alastairreid.github.io/RelatedWork/papers/mcmillan:sigcomm:2019); [Galois](https://www.galois.com/articles/quic-testing-a-quick-replication)). Usability decides adoption as much as power does: Verum's spreadsheet-like editor for ASD was "a substantial obstacle" to acceptance, and AWS kept specification snippets out of its paper because "their unfamiliar syntax can be off-putting" ([Dezyne](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.02962); [Newcombe et al.](https://lamport.azurewebsites.net/tla/formal-methods-amazon.pdf)).

### AI fits propose-then-check, but nothing checks what a specification means

Across every ecosystem the same pattern has emerged: a model proposes a specification, repair, invariant or proof, and an existing checker decides. Event-B Agent repairs models from Rodin's proof-obligation feedback, PeasyAI generates P state machines and test drivers, Quint Studio authors specifications from code and documentation, and the TLA+ Foundation's 2025 challenge was won by a tool that synthesises TLA+ from source code ([Event-B Agent](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.17475); [P README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/p-org/P/master/README.md); [Quint](https://quint.sh/); [TLA+ Foundation](https://foundation.tlapl.us/blog/2025-08-dev-update/index.html)). Success rates on small, fixed specifications are high and rising: on the 782 DafnyBench tasks success rose from 68% in mid-2024 to **89% for one model and 96% for the union of models**, and off-the-shelf models reach 82% on Dafny specifications in the vericoding benchmark, but only 44% in Verus and 27% in Lean ([Vericoding](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.22908)). AutoVerus reports over 90% on its own benchmark, which a reproduction measured at 75% with prompts tied to one Verus version ([AutoVerus](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.13082); [ExVerus](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.25810)). At system scale the rates fall. In VeruSAGE's no-lemma variant the best model proves 41% of the hardest Verus system project's tasks at about USD 96 per task, and the best agent on TLAPS-Bench proves 31 of 70 targets drawn from real system specifications, including cache-coherence and Byzantine-consensus properties that its authors say had never been proved ([VeruSAGE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.18436); [TLAPS-Bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02849)). A May 2026 preprint jointly synthesises implementation and proof for distributed key-value-store specifications, solving 7 of 7 at about USD 106 each against 2 of 7 for general coding agents, though its abstract does not name the verifier ([IDS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.23109)). An evaluation of 30 LLMs concluded that current models "do not generate reliable TLA+ specifications without expert oversight" ([AI4FM](https://ai4fm.cs.luc.edu/papers/llm-tla-evaluation/)).

**The strongest results put the model inside a symbolic loop.** IC3Syn's own ablation drops from 29 to 23 protocols when the IC3 structure is removed, and its authors report that asking for a whole inductive invariant "remains ineffective" because any gap in the conjunction invalidates it, so the model proposes one blocking clause at a time ([Cao et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.24619)). Its hardest result, an industrial Raft reconfiguration protocol no baseline solved, came with a TLAPS proof of more than 3,000 lines written "mainly by agents with minimal human intervention" ([Cao et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.24619v1)). Hardware AI is earlier and vendor-led: Cadence's Jasper AI Assistant generates SystemVerilog assertions under expert review, and Cadence claims its ChipStack agent cuts a typical five-week verification process to under a day, a claim not independently verified ([DVCon](https://dvcon-proceedings.org/wp-content/uploads/194-SIGMA-Sign-off-Intelligence-with-GenAI-for-Methodical-Assurance-in-Formal-Verification.pdf); [engineering.com](https://www.engineering.com/cadence-extends-chip-design-agent-to-level-5-autonomy/)). **No published AI system was found producing kernel-checked refinement proofs for RTL, and no work has a model propose refinement mappings, simulation relations or abstraction functions between hardware and protocol layers.**

What no checker covers is meaning. Proofs are trusted only as far as the checker, and benchmarks now guard against models that weaken the goal: AutoVerus rejects candidates that change code or specifications as "unsafe", AlphaVerus filters "misaligned specifications and programs to prevent reward hacking", and vericoding scans for attempts to disable the checker ([AutoVerus](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.13082); [AlphaVerus](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.06176); [Vericoding](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.22908)). Whether a specification says what was intended is left to people. The IMO 2025 problem statements behind Aristotle's Lean proofs were translated into Lean by hand, and SysMoBench scores AI-written TLA+ against the real system's code and invariants, not only for syntax ([Aristotle](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.01346); [SysMoBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.23130)). A kernel can absorb untrusted proposals of proofs, invariants and sub-obligations from any source; specification fidelity, vacuity and conformance to the implementation lie outside what any kernel checks, and no surveyed system closes that gap.

## Eight problems no existing system solves together

The four traditions converge on a set of problems, each partly solved somewhere and none solved together. They are open problems with the pieces that come closest; which to take on, and in what order, is for the team to decide.

| Open problem | Closest pieces today | What is missing |
|---|---|---|
| One semantics across the clock boundary | Kami's method labels; Kôika's schedules; mCRL2's multi-actions; TLA+'s joint-action specs | A semantics stating clocked fixed-width circuits and asynchronous, faulty, every-N protocols together, with a proved relation between clocked and interleaved composition |
| Refinement from an N-instance circuit to an every-N protocol | Kami's replication theorem; cutoff inference in IC3PO; Cubicle for coherence protocols ([Cubicle](https://cubicle.lmf.cnrs.fr/papers/fmcad2013.pdf)) | A checked link from a cutoff or replication argument to a concrete RTL instance |
| Certificates with theories, quantifiers and liveness | LRAT; witness circuits; Alethe; cvc5's format with trust steps | Small-kernel evidence for word-level, quantified first-order, liveness and failures-refinement results |
| Checkers free of unverified solvers | cake_lpr; verified LRAT checkers; CAVA | Witness checks replayed in a kernel with every SAT query backed by a checked proof |
| Trusted front and back ends | Kôika's verified compiler; Lutsig; seL4's binary validation | Checked elaboration from source text to the kernel's object, and from it to what is fabricated |
| Liveness and timing across layers | IronFleet and Anvil liveness; CertiKOS progress; LVR; Kôika's cycles | Any end-to-end progress or timing claim from circuit to protocol |
| Specification meaning | Equivalence proofs between independent specs; conformance suites; trace validation; PObserve | Checks of fidelity, vacuity and conformance; proposals of cross-layer refinement mappings |
| Cost | Armada's proof generators; Kondo; Basilisk; IC3Syn; EPR islands | Automation that emits checkable steps for invariants and refinement mappings at system scale |

The problems are coupled. The first two are semantic and decide what the certificates in the third must certify, and because parameterized model checking is undecidable in general, the second will always rest on restricted classes or invented invariants ([Mazur & Lowe](https://arxiv.org/abs/1201.1716)). Keeping verification conditions in a decidable fragment eases the second and the last, as Ivy and Verus's EPR islands show, but narrows what the first can express ([Ivy](http://microsoft.github.io/ivy/decidability.html); [Verus 2024](https://www.andrew.cmu.edu/user/bparno/papers/verus-sys.pdf)). The fourth and fifth are closer to engineering, with working demonstrations of each part: a machine-code-verified LRAT checker, a verified synthesis tool, a verified HDL compiler and binary-level translation validation ([Tan, Heule, Myreen](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7984575/); [Lööw](https://www.doc.ic.ac.uk/~aloow/papers/thesis.pdf); [Kôika](https://people.csail.mit.edu/bthom/pldi20.pdf); [seL4 FAQ](https://sel4.systems/About/FAQ.html)). The sixth and seventh have no complete demonstration anywhere, and the authors who brought certificates to the hardware competition call liveness certification "another open research challenge" ([Froleyks et al.](https://cca.informatik.uni-freiburg.de/papers/FroleyksYuPreinerBiereHeljanko-CAV25.pdf)).

History shows where risk concentrates. Verified distributed systems failed at their shims, not in their verified protocols ([Fonseca et al.](https://www.cs.purdue.edu/homes/pfonseca/papers/eurosys2017-dsbugs.pdf)); Lightbulb found specification bugs only when two independently written specifications met ([Erbsen et al.](https://adam.chlipala.net/papers/LightbulbPLDI21/LightbulbPLDI21.pdf)); Arm trusted its specification only after billions of conformance-test instructions ([Reid et al.](https://alastairreid.github.io/papers/cav2016_isa_formal.pdf)). A language whose novelty is a checked refinement across the clock boundary creates a new boundary of exactly the kind where these failures cluster.

Three caveats bound these conclusions. The claim that no unified language exists rests on absence of evidence across the sources searched; industrial ACL2 work and TLA+ use in industrial hardware design were not examined and could qualify it. Commercial engines are opaque: the research found no public description of Jasper's engines and no evidence either way on whether it emits certificates. And many of the 2025–2026 results cited, including IC3Syn, TLAPS-Bench and the system-level AI work, are preprints with self-reported numbers, and no head-to-head comparison on shared benchmarks was found for most tool families.

## Conclusion

The survey changes the shape of the problem more than it narrows the options. The five axes are one decision seen five ways: the observable and its stance on stuttering and divergence decide which compositions preserve refinement, which liveness claims survive and what a certificate must contain, so the order in which those commitments are fixed matters as much as the commitments themselves. Two levers recur independently in hardware and in distributed systems and look transferable across the clock boundary. Limiting each atomic step to one state-changing interaction per component (Fjfj's method rule, IronFleet's receive-then-send, Anvil's one request per step) turns exponential or interleaving-heavy obligations into per-step ones, and stating a deliberately more nondeterministic contract, then proving it harmless in context, keeps modular proofs possible. Both rest on a soundness theorem, reduction or substitution, that a checker-only regime has to prove once and reuse; Armada and Fjfj show this is feasible, and IronFleet shows how easily it is skipped.

The trust picture inverts a common assumption. Small, trustworthy kernels are well understood; the residual risk lives in specifications, translators, environment models and layer boundaries, and the missing certificates are exactly the ones that every-N and word-level proofs need, for theories, quantifiers and liveness. A cross-layer language therefore places its novelty where existing systems are weakest. AI is already changing the economics of the dominant manual cost, inventing invariants and proof steps, but it moves none of the trust: whatever it proposes is either checked by the kernel or, for the meaning of the specification itself, checked by nothing yet.
